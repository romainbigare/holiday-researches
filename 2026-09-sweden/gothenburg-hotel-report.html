Gothenburg, re-scored for grandeur

You redefined luxury: not spas and pools, but centrality, aesthetic quality, grand design, big rooms and interiors that have been looked after. So I threw out the first index and built a new one, and went and got the number that actually decides this — the floor area of the room. It turns out to be the most badly behaved variable in the city. You can pay £200 a night for 10 m², or £195 for 30 m².

The short answer

On your revised definition of luxury.

Avalon Hotel — £313.51 for the two nights, £156.76 a night, breakfast in.

It is the only hotel in the city with no weak leg against any of your four criteria. 120 m from Kungsportsplatsen, the most central address of anything worth staying in. It was Gothenburg’s first Design Hotels member: art deco against sixties classics, 24-carat gold mosaic, white marble, a serious art collection. Rooms are 20 m² with rainforest showers. And it holds 9.0 on Booking and 9.6 on Expedia — the best-maintained property in the entire design-and-grandeur tier.

If “grand” to you means period grandeur rather than design, take Elite Plaza instead at £145.24 a night — £11.52 a night cheaper. It is an 1889 palace with every original feature kept: stucco ceilings, English mosaic floors, marble stairs. It is the single most architecturally impressive building you can sleep in here for under £200. The trade is 18 m² rooms rather than 20, and a slightly lower upkeep score.

What this costs you. Your £100 budget does not survive contact with these criteria. Grand design starts at £145 a night in this city. At £100 you are buying a 17–19 m² room in a plain hotel. The gap between the two is about £95 across the two nights — roughly one good dinner.

The two traps

Scandic No. 25 sells a 8 m² standard double at £115.39 a night. Hotel Pigalle is a five-star charging £200.35 a night whose entry “Petite” room is 10 m². Both would read as reasonable on any star rating or review score. Neither is a room you want to spend a honeymoon evening in.

The one genuine outlier

Radisson Blu Scandinavia gives you 30 m² as standard — two and a half times a Hotel Royal room, and the largest in the city at any price short of a Dorsia suite. At £195.27 it is expensive, and the building is a modern atrium rather than a grand facade. If floor area is what you mean by luxurious, this is the answer.

What changed from the first pass, and why

The index was rebuilt

The first version gave 25 of 100 points to pools, saunas and spas — the heaviest single weight. That is now gone entirely. The replacement: grand design 30, room size 25, interior upkeep 25, centrality 20. Centrality is also re-anchored: it used to measure distance to Haga and Järntorget, the cool districts. It now measures distance to Kungsportsplatsen, the actual centre, because you asked for central rather than trendy.

Room size and centrality are measured. Upkeep is the guest score adjusted for what reviewers specifically say about room condition. Design is my judgement, and it is the one you might score differently.

Three properties moved a long way

  • Elite Park Avenue fell from first to second. It won last time chiefly on the pool and spa, which you have now discounted to zero.
  • Hotel Royal was fifth and “most charming”. Its rooms are 13 m², which I did not know then. It still tops the value index, but I can no longer recommend the standard rate.
  • Avalon was sixth and dismissed as too expensive. Under the new weights it has the best balance in the city, and it is now the pick.

Two hotels also entered the field that the first pass never surfaced: Clarion Hotel Post and Radisson Blu Scandinavia.

One finding worth stating plainly

Almost every grand old hotel in Gothenburg draws “dated” complaints from guests — Elite Plaza, Eggers, Clarion Post and Pigalle all do, and all four lack air conditioning. This is a structural feature of the city’s historic hotel stock, not bad luck in the sample. Only two properties combine period or design character with genuinely well-maintained interiors: Dorsia at £272.61 and Avalon at £156.76. That is the real reason the pick is what it is. The air conditioning, incidentally, does not matter on your dates — Gothenburg averages about 19 °C in early September.

Before you book

Three phone calls worth making

  • Hotel Flora’s 30 m² superior. Not priced online. If it lands near £150 it beats everything on this page: biggest room, best address.
  • Hotel Royal’s 20 m² superior. Turns the best-value hotel from unbookable into a real contender.
  • Elite Plaza’s fifth floor. Reviewers say those rooms are materially better — skyline views, bath and shower.

Still true from the first pass

  • The car goes back Monday, so ignore every parking charge on these listings.
  • Wednesday’s 13:30 flight: the airport coach runs every 20 minutes from Nils Ericson (30 min) and Korsvägen (20 min). Eggers is 130 m from the terminal; Avalon is 660 m.
  • Check cancellation terms — I did not filter for free cancellation, so the cheapest rate shown may be prepaid.
  • You are booking one day out. These are today’s live rates and several moved overnight.
