First, what inland actually looks like

Worth setting expectations before the recommendations.

You asked for forests, lakes, dramatic landscape and traditional villages. You will get three of those four easily. Inland Västergötland and Dalsland are genuinely forested, genuinely full of lakes, and full of red timber villages that look the way people picture Sweden.

Dramatic is the one to adjust. There are no mountains here. The highest ground you will stand on is about 300 metres. What you get instead is Tiveden, which is a forest full of house-sized boulders and split cliffs left by the ice — strange and genuinely impressive at ground level, but not a view over a valley. Kinnekulle is a flat-topped hill with limestone terraces. Vättern and Vänern are inland seas: Vänern is the largest lake in the European Union and you cannot see across either of them.

So: quieter and greener than the coast, cheaper by a clear margin, and with far more saunas. Less immediately spectacular. The trade is real in both directions.

The short answer

What the drive-time rules leave standing.

Base yourself on the west shore of Vättern, between Hjo and Karlsborg.

This is the only inland area that puts the dramatic bit and a proper old town within your day-trip rule at the same time. Tiveden National Park is 41 minutes from Karlsborg and 55 from Hjo. Hjo itself is a preserved 19th-century wooden town on the lake. Hornborgasjön is an hour. The airport run is 2 hours to Hjo and 2 h 13 to the Karlsborg cabin, both inside your standard allowance.

My pick is the lakeside cottage at Fagersanna, £303 for the three nights (£101 a night). It sits on the shore of Lake Örlen with its own beach and private garden, every review is five stars, and every category scores a clean 5.0. Tiveden is 45 minutes away.

One warning worth reading before you fall in love with it. The best-equipped place inland — a cabin called Ugglan with its own wood-fired hot tub and private sauna, 25 minutes from Tiveden — is at Undenäs, and Undenäs is 2 h 36 from the airport. That is six minutes past the ceiling you set. It is on the map and in the list so you can decide for yourself.

What to know before you book

How solid these numbers are

  • Prices are live Airbnb quotes for 28–31 August 2026, two guests, in GBP, fees included. They move, and several showed short-term discounts.
  • Ratings are as published on 24 August 2026. Where a place has under 20 reviews I have said so.
  • Drive times are Google Maps, free-flow, no traffic. Only the ones in the table above were checked individually; the times on the cards for other properties are estimates from their area.
  • Amenities come from each listing's own amenity list. Private versus shared sauna is the host's wording, not mine.

Things I could not confirm

  • Tiveden's visitor centre and café at Stenkälla usually close for the season in late August. The park and trails stay open. Worth checking before you drive up.
  • Restaurants are much thinner inland than on the coast, and many rural ones shut after the Swedish holidays end. Assume you are cooking most nights.
  • Läckö Castle and Kinnekulle both run reduced opening in September. Check dates before planning a day around either.
  • Two listings show no bathroom and one shows no heating. I have flagged those individually, but confirm with the host before booking in September.
  • No hosts were contacted. Everything here comes from the listings themselves.
