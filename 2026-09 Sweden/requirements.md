# Trip parameters — Option D

Research brief for a five-night trip to West Sweden, 28 August – 2 September 2026.
Place names deliberately omitted; these are the constraints and decision rules only.

---

## 1. Fixed facts

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Travellers | 2 adults, one room |
| Origin | London |
| Occasion | Mini honeymoon |
| Arrival | Friday 28 August 2026, lands **17:00** |
| Departure | Wednesday 2 September 2026, departs **13:30** |
| Nights available | **5** — 28, 29, 30, 31 Aug and 1 Sep |
| Arrival/departure airport | Same airport, ~35 min from the city |
| Reporting currency | **GBP** |
| FX used | £1 = SEK 12.89 · $1 = SEK 9.53 |

---

## 2. The constraint that drives the whole shape

The arrival weekend is the single most expensive weekend of the destination city's
late summer: a city-wide festival plus two consecutive stadium concert nights, with
the university term starting the following Monday.

**Measured effect:** cheapest available city hotel was **£272/night** on the arrival
weekend against **£64/night** the following midweek — a **4.2× premium**. Like-for-like
on a single property was roughly 4×.

**Decision rule derived:** do not sleep in the city on arrival. Drive out the same
evening, spend the expensive nights in the countryside, and return to the city midweek.

**Value of that single decision:** ~£400 on lodging across the trip.

---

## 3. Trip shape

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Structure | **3 nights rural + 2 nights city** |
| Order | Rural first, city second (forced by §2) |
| Number of hotels | **2 total** — one rural base, one city hotel |
| Rural bases | **1** (no moving mid-leg; this is the defining feature of Option D) |
| City nights | Monday and Tuesday — cheapest of the week |
| Checkout | Wednesday morning; last morning is travel only |

**Rejected alternatives and why**
- 4 rural + 1 city — one city night is not worth unpacking for.
- 2 rural + 3 city — wastes the cheap countryside and buys expensive city nights.
- 2+ rural bases — repacking mid-trip on a 5-night honeymoon; rejected on feel, not logistics.

---

## 4. Timing constraints

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Sunset on arrival day | ~20:20, losing ~4 min/day |
| Usable Friday departure | ~18:15 (17:00 landing + bags + car collection) |
| **Day 1 drive allowance** | **Up to 2 h as standard; up to 2 h 30 if the destination is genuinely scenic** |
| **Final day drive allowance** | **Up to 2 h as standard; up to 2 h 30 acceptable** |
| Max day-trip drive from base | ~1 h 10 each way |
| Wednesday airport arrival | 11:30 for a 13:30 departure |

**Day 1 daylight trade-off.** A 2 h drive arrives around 20:15, just inside sunset.
A 2 h 30 drive arrives around 20:45, after dark. Accepting the longer drive means
trading the first evening's views and a relaxed dinner for a better base — worth it
if the destination genuinely earns it, not otherwise. Dinner should be assumed to be
at the accommodation on the first night either way.

**Final day departure times.** Leave the last night's base by 09:30 for a 2 h drive,
or 09:00 for 2 h 30. Either way the final morning is travel only — do not plan
anything into it.

**What the relaxation unlocks.** At 90 minutes the last night was effectively forced
into the city, and the search radius for a base was capped tighter than the day-trip
rule required. At 2–2 h 30 both open up: a rural base can sit further out, and a rural
final night becomes geographically possible. Note that a rural final night still runs
into the separate objection in §3 — a single city night is not worth unpacking for —
so this widens the search area rather than changing the 3-rural/2-city shape. Section 2
is unaffected: wherever the city nights fall, they must not be the arrival weekend.

## 6. Base selection criteria

Ranked. Criterion 1 is a hard requirement, not a tiebreak — an earlier iteration
optimised for position and produced a base that was well-located but visually plain,
which was rejected.

1. **Visual charm — postcard quality.** The base must itself be attractive to look at
   and to walk around. Working ports, industrial towns and post-war development fail
   this even when well positioned.
2. **Position.** All intended day trips reachable in ≤ ~1 h 10 each way, in both
   directions from the base (i.e. it must sit between the two clusters of interest).
3. **Architecture and design of the property.**
4. **Isolation and views** from the room.
5. **Sauna / water access** — strong bonus, not required.
6. **Guest rating** as a tiebreak.

**Accepted trade:** ~20 minutes added to each day trip in exchange for a materially
prettier base. Judged worth it for a honeymoon.

## 7. Landscape and experience criteria

- Dramatic **or** very green. Both acceptable; mixed is ideal.
- Coastal rock, open sea, and water access.
- Walks and hikes available directly from or near the base.
- Small villages for coffee and lunch.
- **No tourist crowds** — this is a primary filter, not a preference.
- One memorable on-water experience budgeted (~£50–90 per person).
- Cold-water swimming: sea ~18°C in late August.

| Ferries | Ordinary public transport pricing (£3–6 typical), not tourist-boat pricing |
| City leg | No car; walkable plus tram and ferry on one ticket |

## 9. Seasonality

- Travelling in the **shoulder week** immediately after the domestic school holidays end.
- Crowds are genuinely low from ~1 September; this is the reason for the dates working.
- Room rates drop measurably between late August and early September (~15–25% observed).
- **Risk:** rural cafés, activity operators and small museums often close after mid-August.
  Anything the itinerary depends on must be confirmed by phone or email the week before.
- Hotels, main restaurants, ferries and trails run as normal.

## 10. Research and evidence rules

- Prices must be **date-specific and live** for the actual nights — not generic or seasonal averages.
- Every figure labelled by confidence:
  - **published** — stated by the property itself
  - **live quote** — returned by a booking system for these exact dates
  - **aggregator** — a real rate, but for a nearby sample date
  - **ask** — not published anywhere; requires direct contact
- All output in GBP, with the source currency and FX rate stated.
- Estimates never presented as quotes.
- Distances and drive times cross-checked rather than taken from a single source.
- Absence of evidence stated as such, not reported as a negative finding.
